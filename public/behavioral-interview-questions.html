What Are Behavioural Interview Questions?

Behavioural interview questions ask you to describe specific situations from your past to predict how you will behave in the future. They typically start with:

  • "Tell me about a time when..."
  • "Give me an example of..."
  • "Describe a situation where..."
  • "How have you handled..."

Employers use behavioural questions because past behaviour is the most reliable predictor of future performance. They want real stories with specific detail, not theoretical answers about what you would do.

The STAR Method

Every behavioural answer should follow the STAR structure:

S
Situation
Set the context. Where were you, what was the background?
T
Task
What was your specific responsibility in that situation?
A
Action
What did you do? Be specific and focus on your contribution.
R
Result
What happened? Quantify the outcome where possible.

How to Build Your Story Bank

Before your interview, prepare 8–10 core stories that can be adapted across different questions. One strong story about a project you led can answer questions about leadership, problem-solving, conflict, and initiative. Build depth over breadth.

For each story, attach numbers to your results: "increased revenue by 22%", "reduced churn by 15%", "shipped three weeks early", "managed a team of six". Specific outcomes are what separate memorable answers from forgettable ones.

50 Behavioural Interview Questions by Category

Use these to build your story bank before any interview. For each question, practise a full STAR answer out loud.

Leadership & Teamwork

  • Tell me about a time you led a team through a difficult situation.What they want: Leadership under pressure, decision-making, how you keep a team motivated when things are hard.
  • Describe a time you had to work with a difficult team member.What they want: Conflict resolution, professionalism, collaboration skills.
  • Tell me about a time you motivated a team.What they want: Leadership style, emotional intelligence, ability to influence without authority.
  • Give an example of when you had to delegate tasks.What they want: Trust in others, task prioritisation, management skills.
  • Describe a time you disagreed with a team decision.What they want: How you handle disagreement professionally and advocate for your point of view.
  • Tell me about a successful team project you contributed to.What they want: Collaboration, your specific role and impact on team success.
  • Describe a time you had to give difficult feedback to a colleague.What they want: Communication skills, empathy, directness.
  • Tell me about a time you helped resolve a team conflict.What they want: Mediation skills, objectivity, problem-solving.
  • Describe a time you had to work with a cross-functional team.What they want: Adaptability, communication across departments, coordination skills.
  • Tell me about a time you built trust with a new team.What they want: Relationship-building, self-awareness, interpersonal effectiveness.

Problem-Solving & Decision-Making

  • Tell me about a complex problem you solved.What they want: Analytical thinking, creativity, your problem-solving approach.
  • Describe a time you had to make a decision without complete information.What they want: Judgment under uncertainty, calculated risk-taking.
  • Tell me about a time you identified a problem before others did.What they want: Proactivity, attention to detail, foresight.
  • Give an example of a creative solution you developed.What they want: Innovation, thinking outside conventional approaches.
  • Describe a time you had to analyse data to make a decision.What they want: Data literacy, analytical skills, evidence-based thinking.
  • Tell me about a time you had to pivot your approach mid-project.What they want: Flexibility, learning from new information, adaptability.
  • Describe a time you predicted a problem and prevented it.What they want: Proactive thinking, risk management.
  • Tell me about a time you simplified a complex process.What they want: Efficiency mindset, process improvement skills.
  • Give an example of when you made a difficult decision quickly.What they want: Decision speed, confidence under pressure.
  • Describe a time you challenged the status quo.What they want: Initiative, critical thinking, courage to speak up.

Failure & Learning

  • Tell me about a time you failed.What they want: Ownership of mistakes, learning mindset, resilience.
  • Describe a time you made a mistake at work.What they want: Honesty, how you handled the consequences.
  • Tell me about a project that didn't go as planned.What they want: How you handle setbacks and recover.
  • Give an example of constructive criticism you received and acted on.What they want: Openness to feedback, growth mindset.
  • Describe a time you missed a deadline.What they want: Accountability and how you communicated the issue.
  • Tell me about a time you received negative feedback.What they want: Emotional maturity, receptiveness to criticism.
  • Give an example of when you changed your approach after a setback.What they want: Adaptability, willingness to change course.
  • Describe a time you took responsibility for a team failure.What they want: Leadership accountability, ownership.
  • Tell me about your biggest professional regret.What they want: Self-reflection, honesty, what you learned.
  • Describe a time you had to recover from a significant setback.What they want: Resilience, perseverance, recovery ability.

Communication & Influence

  • Tell me about a time you had to persuade someone.What they want: Influence skills, argumentation, negotiation ability.
  • Describe a time you explained something complex to a non-technical audience.What they want: Communication clarity, empathy for your audience.
  • Tell me about a time you handled a difficult customer or stakeholder.What they want: De-escalation, customer service, conflict management.
  • Give an example of when you delivered difficult news.What they want: Difficult conversations, honesty, tact.
  • Describe a time you influenced a decision without formal authority.What they want: Soft power, influence through ideas and relationships.
  • Tell me about a presentation you gave that had a real impact.What they want: Public speaking, preparation, clarity of message.
  • Describe a time you had to build consensus across a disagreement.What they want: Facilitation, bringing people together.
  • Give an example of when you used data to support an argument.What they want: Evidence-based persuasion, credibility.
  • Describe a time you changed someone's mind.What they want: Persuasion strategy, empathy, listening.
  • Tell me about a time you communicated across cultural or language barriers.What they want: Cultural sensitivity, adaptability, patience.

Achievement & Initiative

  • Tell me about your greatest professional achievement.What they want: Pride in work, impact, and what you value.
  • Describe a time you went above and beyond what was expected.What they want: Extra effort, dedication, initiative.
  • Tell me about a time you took initiative without being asked.What they want: Proactivity, ownership, self-motivation.
  • Give an example of a project you are most proud of.What they want: Your values, what drives you professionally.
  • Describe a time you exceeded expectations.What they want: High standards, overdelivering.
  • Tell me about a time you improved a process or system.What they want: Innovation, efficiency mindset.
  • Describe a time you identified a new business opportunity.What they want: Business acumen, opportunity recognition.
  • Give an example of when you showed persistence to achieve a goal.What they want: Grit, determination, follow-through.
  • Tell me about a time you delivered results under significant pressure.What they want: Performance under stress, prioritisation, composure.
  • Describe a time you learned a new skill quickly to meet a challenge.What they want: Learning agility, growth mindset, adaptability.

Common Questions

What are behavioural interview questions?
Behavioural interview questions ask you to describe specific past situations to predict future behaviour. They start with phrases like "Tell me about a time..." or "Give me an example of..." Employers use them because past behaviour is the strongest predictor of future performance.
How many stories do I need to prepare?
8 to 10 core stories is ideal. Each story should be adaptable to multiple question types — a single story about a major project can answer questions on leadership, problem-solving, conflict, and achievement. Depth beats breadth: a few well-developed stories are more useful than a long list of thin ones.
What if I don't have a strong example for a question?
Use the closest thing you have and be honest about the scale. "In my current role as a junior analyst, I don't yet lead teams, but here's how I navigated a situation where I disagreed with a senior colleague..." is a perfectly good answer. Interviewers at senior levels care about quality; interviewers at junior levels care about potential and self-awareness.
