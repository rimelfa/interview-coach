How to Answer "Why Should We Hire You?" in an Interview

Last updated: March 2026 • 7 min read
"Why should we hire you?" is your moment to close the deal. It's usually asked late in the interview when they're comparing you to other candidates. Most people fumble by being too modest or by listing generic qualities everyone claims. This guide shows you how to make a compelling case that separates you from the competition.

What This Question Really Asks

When interviewers ask "Why should we hire you?", they're actually asking:

  • "What makes you different from the other qualified candidates?"
  • "Can you solve our specific problems?"
  • "Are you confident in your abilities without being arrogant?"
  • "Have you paid attention to what we need?"

This is your chance to directly address why you're the best choice. It's not about being modest or humble—it's about making a clear, confident argument for why hiring you is the smart decision.

The Framework: Match + Proof + Unique Value

1. Match Their Needs (20 seconds)

Reference the specific requirements or challenges they've mentioned.

  • Show you've been listening
  • Connect to what they actually need
  • Be specific, not generic

2. Provide Proof (30 seconds)

Give concrete examples that demonstrate you can deliver.

  • Share relevant achievements
  • Use numbers and results
  • Show you've done this before

3. Add Your Unique Value (20 seconds)

What do you bring that others don't?

  • Unique skill combinations
  • Relevant industry knowledge
  • Cultural fit and enthusiasm

Example Answers

Example 1: Software Engineer

"You should hire me because I have exactly the combination of skills you need for this role—strong backend development experience plus the ability to think about business impact.

At my current company, I built the payment processing system that now handles $2M in transactions monthly with 99.9% uptime. I also worked directly with the product team to shape features that increased conversion by 18%. That's the kind of technical execution combined with business thinking you mentioned needing.

What makes me different is that I don't just write code—I ship products that move metrics. And from everything we've discussed, that's exactly what this role requires at this stage of your growth."

Example 2: Marketing Manager

"You should hire me because you need someone who can generate pipeline in a competitive B2B market, and I've proven I can do exactly that.

In my last role, I grew our marketing qualified leads by 200% in 18 months while reducing cost per lead by 35%. I did it by rebuilding our content strategy, launching a partner program, and optimizing our paid channels. Those are the same levers you mentioned needing to pull here.

What sets me apart is my experience in your specific industry—SaaS for healthcare. I understand the compliance challenges, the long sales cycles, and what resonates with your buyers. I can hit the ground running on day one."

Example 3: Entry-Level / Early Career

"You should hire me because while I may have less experience than other candidates, I make up for it with resourcefulness and speed of learning.

During my internship, I was given a basic data entry project, but I automated the entire process with Python scripts, which saved the team 15 hours per week. I taught myself Python specifically for that project. That's how I approach challenges—I find solutions and I figure it out.

What makes me a great fit is my hunger to prove myself. I know I have to earn my place here, and I'm willing to put in the work. Based on our conversation, I'm excited about this team and the problems you're solving, and I want to be part of it."

How to Build Your Answer

  1. Review the job description: What are the top 3 requirements?
  2. Note what they emphasized: What problems or challenges did they mention in the interview?
  3. Match your strengths to their needs: Where do your skills perfectly align?
  4. Pick your best proof points: What achievements best demonstrate these abilities?
  5. Identify your differentiator: What makes you unique compared to typical candidates?

Do's and Don'ts

✓ DO

  • Reference their specific needs
  • Use concrete examples and numbers
  • Sound confident, not arrogant
  • Show enthusiasm for the role
  • Differentiate yourself
  • Keep it under 90 seconds

✗ DON'T

  • Give generic answers anyone could say
  • Just list your skills without proof
  • Be overly modest or uncertain
  • Focus only on what you want to gain
  • Repeat what's on your resume
  • Compare yourself negatively to others

Common Mistakes

  • The Generic Answer: "I'm hardworking, reliable, and a team player." So is everyone else.
  • The Resume Repeat: "I have 5 years of experience and a degree in..." They know this already.
  • The Humble Deflection: "I'm not sure why you should hire me over others..." You just killed your chances.
  • The Desperate Plea: "I really need this job and would be so grateful..." They're hiring for skills, not pity.
  • The Rambler: Talking for 5 minutes without making a clear point.

Variations of This Question

You might hear this question phrased differently, but the answer strategy is the same:

  • "What makes you the best candidate for this position?"
  • "Why are you the right fit for this role?"
  • "What sets you apart from other candidates?"
  • "Why should we choose you?"
